Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurobiological condition marked by difficulty sustaining attention, impulsivity, and, in some cases, hyperactivity.

Contrary to the myth, it isn't just a "childhood thing." In 60–70% of children with ADHD, symptoms persist into adulthood — only the form changes: outward hyperactivity more often turns inward, into a restless, wound-up feeling.

An ADHD brain works differently: it runs with lower dopamine and norepinephrine — neurotransmitters responsible for motivation, impulse control, and the sense of satisfaction. **This isn't a matter of laziness or weak willpower; it's a physiological trait.**

**What follows from that.** Techniques built on "just get it together" don't work here, on principle — not because the person isn't trying hard enough, but because they're aimed at the wrong mechanism. The rest of this knowledge base is about what works instead.

If you'd like to see how closely this matches your own experience, the clinical tests section has the World Health Organization's ASRS-v1.1 screening scale. A screening doesn't give a diagnosis — only a psychiatrist can do that.

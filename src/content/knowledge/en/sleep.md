Sleep debt on its own produces distractibility, impulsiveness, and emotional instability — in other words, it looks like ADHD. Layered onto actual ADHD, it doubles the effect.

It's also a closed loop: hard to stop and go to bed in the evening (delayed sleep phase shows up more often with ADHD), hard to get up in the morning, low on energy in the afternoon.

**What actually works:**

- **The same wake-up time**, even on weekends. Wake time sets your rhythm more strongly than bedtime does.
- **Light in the morning**, in the first half hour after waking — the strongest signal your internal clock gets.
- **A wind-down ritual.** The problem usually isn't sleepiness — it's not being able to stop what you're doing. An external cue helps: a "start winding down" alarm an hour before bed.
- **Dump your head before bed.** If thoughts are circling, write them down instead of trying to resolve them.

Before changing anything complicated, look at sleep first. "Dynamics" shows how it connects to your focus.

title: Deadlines work, but they're expensive
summary: Why everything gets done the last night, and what that costs.
minutes: 2
---

Urgency is one of ADHD attention's four triggers, and the most reliable one. So a lot of things genuinely do get done the last night — and done reasonably well.

The problem is the price: the adrenaline mode wrecks sleep, spikes anxiety, and leaves no room for error. A strategy that works once a month becomes destructive when it's the only one you use.

**How to get urgency without the fire:**

- **Artificial deadlines with an outside witness.** "I'll send my colleague a draft on Wednesday" works; "I'll finish it by Wednesday" doesn't.
- **Break it down to a size that feels urgent.** Not "project by Friday" — "section by 2pm."
- **Work alongside someone.** Agreeing to work at the same time as someone else creates gentle urgency without the threat.

The goal isn't to stop using urgency — it's to keep it small and on your terms.

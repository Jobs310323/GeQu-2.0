category: emotions
title: Accumulated shame
summary: Where "something is wrong with me" comes from, and what to do about it.
minutes: 2
---

By adulthood, someone with ADHD has heard thousands of remarks: "you could do better," "forgot again," "just get it together." Over time, that becomes an inner voice that fires before the outer one does.

The side effect: every failure confirms the old conclusion, and every success gets written off as luck. That's called discounting the positive, and it reliably keeps self-esteem down regardless of actual results.

**What works:**

- **Separate the act from the person.** "I forgot the meeting" instead of "I'm irresponsible."
- **Count what got done, not what got missed.** This is where trackers earn their keep: they hold the facts, and memory doesn't hand them over on a bad day.
- **Talk to yourself like a friend.** You wouldn't say to a friend what you say to yourself. Write the reply you'd give them — and address it to yourself.

Shame doesn't motivate. It slows you down: the worse someone thinks of themselves, the harder it gets to start anything.

title: An interest deficit, not a willpower deficit
summary: Why the things that grab you get done, and the things that "should" get done don't.
minutes: 3
---

With ADHD, the problem isn't how much attention you have — it's **what switches it on**. A neurotypical brain's attention runs on importance: "this matters, so I do it." ADHD attention runs on four other things instead:

- **Interest** — genuinely curious about it
- **Novelty** — it's new or unfamiliar
- **Challenge** — there's competition or difficulty involved
- **Urgency** — the deadline is breathing down your neck

That produces a paradox that looks like laziness from the outside: eight hours straight on an absorbing project, and total inability to send one email.

**What to do about it.** Don't try to make yourself "want" a boring task — artificially add one of the four triggers instead:

- Set a timer — urgency appears
- Do the task differently than usual — novelty appears
- Work alongside someone — a mild challenge appears
- Tie the task to something interesting — interest appears

This isn't a trick you're playing on yourself. It's working with how your attention actually switches on.
